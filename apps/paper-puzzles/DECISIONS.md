# DECISIONS.md — Paper Pines

| Date | Decision | Why | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-13 | First app of the factory is the daily paper puzzle | Earns on both stores (Android ads, iPhone unlock), can be tested on an Android phone, levels are made by code, and the core promise can be proven by tests | Claude ("you decide") |
| 2026-11-13 | First and only puzzle type in v1: Tents | Three rules a person learns in a minute, one-thumb taps, small grids that suit a phone, a solver whose steps can be explained in a sentence | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | A puzzle is kept only if the logic solver finishes it | Gives "one answer, never a guess" and means a hint can always explain the next step | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | Hints are free and unlimited | 565 reviews: coin and pack paywalls are hated; explained hints are asked for. This replaces the brief's "one free hint a day, then a video" | Claude, from the review research |
| 2026-11-13 | No timer on screen, no lives; time is shown only after solving | Reviews: lives and pressure are hated | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | Largest grid is 10 × 10 | Bigger squares would be under the 44 pt tap size on a phone | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | Look: Sunday newspaper in two inks, SOLVED stamp as the signature | Different from every Tents app found and from Gobblo; fits "calm" | Claude; Anass can change it |
| 2026-11-13 | Works in Expo Go for now (no native-only libraries); simple screen switch, no Expo Router yet | Anass can test on his phone today without a build | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | AsyncStorage for saves (not MMKV) | Works in Expo Go and on the web preview | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | Working title "Paper Pines" | A web search found nothing with that name; full check still to do | Claude |
| 2026-11-13 | The newspaper look is approved | "Yes, it looks like a newspaper." Seen on screenshots; still to be judged on his phone | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Calendar weeks start on Monday, also in the US | The puzzle week builds from a small Monday puzzle to the big Sunday one, like a newspaper | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | All past days are open for now | The pay-once unlock and the Android video for old days come with the money work; no gate is built before then | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Sounds are made by our own script, not recorded or downloaded | We own them fully and can retune them; they must still be judged by ear on a phone | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Sounds respect the silent switch and mix with the player's music | A puzzle tick must never cut off a podcast | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Undo and Hint sit in a bottom bar | Thumb zone; the board stays clear | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Last seven days of the calendar are always free; older days need one video each (Android) or the full edition (iPhone) | Keeps the daily habit free and gives the paid edition and the videos something real | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | If no video is ready, the day opens anyway | A player must never be left waiting for an ad that is not there (a bug Gobblo had) | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | iPhone: the Book is free to puzzle 30; buying is switched off and nothing locks until a real store adapter works | Untested purchase code must not ship, and a lock with no way through it would be a dead end | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | No full-screen ad before Book puzzle 6, in the first 10 minutes, or more than once every 4 minutes; none around the daily puzzle | The reviews: ads in the middle of play are the most hated thing | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | The app no longer runs in Expo Go (the ads library is native); test builds come from GitHub | Needed for ads; Anass gets a real installed app | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Two modes are shown side by side on the front page: the daily puzzle and the level-by-level puzzle book, with a strip of level boxes | "Make two modes: one like Candy Crush, level by level and harder, and one for each day. We need him to stay in the app." Both existed; the levels were not visible enough | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | A solved daily puzzle leads on to the next level | The old screen ended with "come back tomorrow", a dead end | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Daily reminder: opt-in, one note a day, asked once after a solved daily puzzle, and it stops after a week of silence | "You can wait a whole day but you come back." A reminder is the strongest come-back tool, and the reviews punish apps that nag; a note the player chose is welcome, a note that never stops gets the app deleted | Claude; Anass can change the week limit (`REMINDER_DAYS`) |
| 2026-11-14 | The reminder is made on the phone (no push service, no server) | No account, no cost, nothing leaves the phone, and no push entitlement to explain to Apple | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | With VoiceOver or TalkBack on, each square is its own button ("Row 2, column 3, grass") and dragging is switched off | A blind or low-vision player could not play at all before: the whole grid was one label. Paper-puzzle players are often older | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Code shrinking (R8) is switched off in Android release builds | The first test app closed one second after opening. The emulator log showed why: the shrinker removed a part that Google's ads library needs at start (WorkManager's database). Off costs a few MB and removes a whole class of crashes we cannot test without many phones. Look again before the store build | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Every change to packages or `app.json` is opened on an Android emulator by GitHub ("launch test") | The crash above passed every test and the build. Only starting the real app catches it | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | New sounds: soft plucked notes from one scale, no noise, much quieter; a five-second tune when a puzzle is solved | Anass on his phone: the first sounds were "so loud", "low quality", "noisy", "not relaxing", and he wants a short winning tune that is not loud | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Test builds show the ad status in Settings; an ad that fails to load is asked for again after a minute | Anass reached puzzle 8 without seeing an ad and we could not tell why. Before, one failed load meant no ads for the whole session | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | The puzzle book adapts both ways: kinder after many hints, harder after two fast clean solves | "There's an easy-hard wave algorithm based on the person, right? Like the Candy Crush secret sauce." Before, it only ever got easier, so a strong player had the same slow climb as everyone | Anass asked; thresholds are Claude's guess until real numbers exist |
| 2026-11-14 | A stuck player gets the Hint button lit up after 45 seconds; the board itself is never changed | Anass sent a video on how Candy Crush checks progress after every move and makes the level easier or harder. Our puzzles have one answer and no luck, so the honest version is: help comes forward when a player falls behind. The full system (steering the pieces) is written in `playbook/06` A3b for the first game with luck | Anass asked; Claude chose the form |
| 2026-11-14 | Two challenge modes that can be lost: a clock, and a limited pot of ink (tents + N, with N set by the algorithm) | "Add a race mode with a chrono, and one with a limit of clicks like Candy Crush: if he needs 10 clicks we give 10 + N, and the algorithm plays with N." This goes against the earlier "no timer, no lives" choice from the reviews, so the calm modes stay untouched and the challenges are a separate, optional part of the front page | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | A "click" in the ink mode is a pitched tent, not every tap | Grass marks are the player's pencil notes; charging for them would punish careful thinking, the opposite of the goal | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Personal bests: best clean time per grid size, and win runs in the challenges | "A way to add a best score, so it is a challenge of the person against itself." No leaderboard: that would need a server and accounts | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | A compliment after each solve, based on what the player did (time, no hints, last drop of ink), in a grown-up voice | "A good compliment, but not childish. Not the kidsy way of Candy Crush: this app is like a journal, for mature people." | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Icon: the puzzle-page corner (option A of three) | Anass did not like the pine-and-tent icon and asked for paper. A says "paper" and "puzzle" at a glance; B (a red stamp) is bolder but could be read as a holiday or lumber mark; C (paper on black) is hard to read small | Claude; Anass can switch |
| 2026-11-15 | "One pot of ink" is the main game: the lead story at the top of the front page, with a big headline, an ink pot drawing and the pot shown as drops. "Beat the clock" comes second, then today's puzzle | "The ink mode is principal in the game" and "the 2 modes bigger" | Anass |
| 2026-11-15 | The puzzle book is renamed "Free play · no clock, no ink limit" | "The normal one call it free mode" | Anass |
| 2026-11-15 | In a challenge the ink pot is a row of drops that empty (last two red), and the clock is a bar that empties | A small "Ink 7" label was easy to miss; the limit is the whole game, so it must be seen | Claude |
| 2026-11-15 | Full-screen ads now come after a won ink or clock puzzle too, not only in free play; "no ad before 6 puzzles" counts all modes | With ink as the main game, most players would never reach a free-play "Next" tap, so the app would show almost no ads. Never after a loss | Claude |
| 2026-11-15 | After today's puzzle, "Keep going" leads to the next ink pot, not to free play | The main game is ink | Claude |
| 2026-11-16 | A full-screen ad comes only at a good moment: at least 3 games since the app opened (or since the last ad), and the last 2 were wins. Counted in memory, so each opening starts fresh | Anass got an ad on his very first win right after opening the app: "I don't want people when they lose, they are already frustrated, and an ad showing up, it's disgusting." The old quiet start only counted the first opening ever | Anass |
| 2026-11-16 | Two losses then a win: still no ad; it waits for a second win in a row | Anass's message also said "after 2 lose, a win, an ad". Claude disagreed: the player just fought back from frustration and should enjoy that win. Anass can switch it (`AD_WINS_IN_A_ROW = 1`) | Claude |
| 2026-11-16 | The optional videos (more ink, more time) stay as they are | "Keep this reward videos. I like them" | Anass |
| 2026-11-17 | A challenge attempt is saved on the phone (board, ink used, time spent, lost or not) and picked up exactly as it was. Only a win or "Try again" starts it fresh | Anass: "I get out of ink and quit, and I entered the same level again, and the ink resets. That's a glitch people can use." Save format version 9 | Anass |
| 2026-11-21 | The puzzle board fits the screen height too, so it works on an open foldable or tablet turned sideways; a very short window (split screen) scrolls | Android 16 (required by Google Play since Aug 2026) ignores our portrait lock on screens 600dp wide or more, and there the bottom of the board was hidden under the Undo bar with no way to scroll. Google will require this ("Tier 3") for games from Aug 2027 (`research/2026-11-21-foldables-large-screens.md`) | Claude, after Anass asked for a foldables check |
| 2026-11-21 | We do not use the "game" opt-out that keeps the portrait lock on large screens | It ends with Android 17 and Tier 3 anyway; making landscape work now is cheaper than doing it twice | Claude |
| 2026-11-21 | Ink bottles: 3 free for everyone; one bottle = 3 more drops or 30 more seconds on a lost challenge, board kept. iPhone sells 10 for $1.99 and 30 for $4.99 | Anass wanted something to pay for ("50 replays for $5"). Try again is already free, so a replay is worth nothing; what a losing player wants is to keep the board. Android cannot sell from Morocco | Anass approved ("yes build the ink bottle") |
| 2026-11-21 | Bottles have no limit per puzzle; videos stay at 2 | A player who pays or saved bottles should be able to finish. Videos cost us nothing, so they keep a limit | Claude |
