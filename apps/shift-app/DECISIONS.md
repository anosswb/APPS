# DECISIONS.md

| Date | Decision | Why | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-14 | The second app is the Shift app, with features for blue-collar workers | "Shift app and make some features related to blue collar." He works 12-hour shifts himself; the other candidates were in a full store | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Build first: a calendar that cannot lose or shift days, and pay-period hours with payday | The reviews: lost data, drifting dates and missing payday are the real complaints | Claude, from the research |
| 2026-11-14 | Attendance points, shift differential and forced-overtime marks wait for proof from real US workers | No other app has them, but no review asked for them, and the forums could not be read | Claude; Anass can overrule |
| 2026-11-14 | Every date is worked out from the rotation rule on day numbers, never stored per month | "Keeps moving my days from one month to another." A test checks ten years with no drift | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Pay is an estimate with the plain federal rule only (over 40 hours a week at time and a half) | State rules, double time and differential need official pages first; a wrong number destroys trust | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | The calendar must work with no fixed rotation, and rest days come first | "We work 10 days or 3 days… we don't know where the 5 days are going to be." The research assumed rotations; the man who lives it says otherwise | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Overtime percentages are typed in by the user, several of them, never fixed by the app | "Don't make it constant, make it variant. The people put it." It also removes the risk of the app getting a pay rule wrong | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | Wake-up alarm button, daily reminder with motivation, day and food planning go into the plan | His list of what a worker needs | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | The look: alive and modern, nothing like paper | "They need a live one, like a calendar, something good, not like this, like it's from paper." | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | The grey box uses AsyncStorage, one file, no router | It is a test, not the app. The starter's stack applies when the real screens are built | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | Look B (the board) is out; the look comes from A (sky) and C (ring) | "I love A and C, hard to choose between." | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | One job per tab: Today, Calendar, My day, Pay. Few colours and few words on each | "So much colour and text and things at one tab that feel distracting… put each thing in a place." | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | A day nobody marked is "not known yet" and is never guessed; answers that cross such a day carry `sure: false` | His work has no pattern. A wrong "rest in 2 days" at 5 am is worse than "next rest you marked: Monday" | Claude, from Anass's rule |
| 2026-11-14 | A rule's percentage is the EXTRA on top of the normal rate (50 = pays 1.5 times) | "The percentage they get more when they work overpaid hours" | Claude, from his words; check the wording on the screen with him |
| 2026-11-14 | Two rules on the same hour: the higher percentage wins; adding them is a setting | A guess about how most employers pay. Not checked with US workers | Claude (guess) |
| 2026-11-14 | The look is "Daybreak": the sky for Today, the ring on My day, font 1 (Bricolage Grotesque). Dark only | "Yes, 1 + 2 together, font 1." | Anass |
| 2026-11-14 | The grey box screen is replaced by the real screens (Today, Calendar, My day; Pay is a note for now) | He asked for an APK to try before his usage ends. The grey box stays in the git history | Claude, for his request |
| 2026-11-14 | Test package ID `com.anosswb.shiftapp`. Not final: it becomes permanent only when the app goes to a store, after the name check | A build needs an ID | Claude |
| 2026-11-14 | One tap on a day moves it: not known yet → work → rest → not known yet. Work uses the usual shift from "My numbers" | Fastest way to mark days with no pattern; to be judged on his phone | Claude |
| 2026-11-15 | Second pass on the look after he tried the first APK: tighter and more solid, no gradients on days, light and dark mode | "It's just a calendar that looks like shit… premium, manly and good for the eye, with light and dark mode." | Anass |
| 2026-11-15 | The tap rule is written on the screen: 1 tap = work day, 2 taps = rest day, 3 taps = clear | He asked for it | Anass |

| 2026-11-15 | Real help added from the pains research: a "now" line on Today (time to clock in, time left in the shift, sleep by) and a real Pay tab (own rate, own overtime numbers, week and month estimate) | "Add some real features that help." The research is thin (Reddit blocked): `research/ideas/shift-app-pains.md` | Claude, for his request |
| 2026-11-15 | Pay tab uses three of his rule kinds for now: over N hours a week, over N hours a day, Sundays. "Days I mark" (holiday, night) waits for the day editor | Smallest useful step; the engine already does all four | Claude |
| 2026-11-15 | Third pass on the look: calm blue, black and green, soft glass panels, days as a number with a dot, plain pill buttons. Amber, the sunset and the day boxes are gone | On his phone: "those calendar boxes look like shit, colours childish… calm colour like those blue black green UI elements… liquid glass not too much." He sent reference screens; we took patterns, not the screens | Anass |
| 2026-11-15 | A marked work day no longer keeps its own times: it always uses the usual shift from My numbers (`planOf` in `src/store.ts`) | His bug, second time: "My day still constant even if I change the hours." Old saved days kept old times | Anass (bug), Claude (fix) |
| 2026-11-15 | Pay is now a daily log: clock in, clock out, pay for each day, the week on top (new each Sunday) and the month under it. Fixed hours a day, overtime above them | "A place he logs each day when he clocks in and out, type what's fixed hours and you calculate the overtime, every day's pay, and on top monthly and weekly." | Anass |
| 2026-11-15 | Totals count logged days only; marked days not worked yet show as "still planned" in green | So the big number is never money he has not earned | Claude |

| 2026-11-15 | Pay stub check: weekly or every-2-weeks periods; type the stub's hours and gross pay; the app shows the gap in plain words | His pick from the research. No legal words, only "check your days, then ask payroll" | Anass |
