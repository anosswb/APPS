# 20 — Two on one phone
**Game · for two friends with one phone, in the US · first-guess score 26/40 · NOT ready to build: it needs a twist first**

## What it is
Small, fast duels for two people on the same phone, one thumb each: who taps first, who remembers more, who pulls harder.

## Why it waits
There are many two-player apps and we have no advantage here. On the scoring rule (`playbook/02`), Gap and Edge are both under 3, so it does not pass.

## Twists to test (pick one, prove it, or drop the idea)
- **"Loser buys lunch":** the duels keep one running score for the whole break and name the loser at the end. A game for the break room and the diner booth.
- **Break-room duels:** tiny games from working life (stack the pallet first, back the trailer in straight).
- **For a parent and a child together** (careful: this moves towards children's rules; probably no).

## If a twist passes: what version 1 holds
- Four duels, each learned in five seconds.
- An evening score, shown big at the end, to share as a picture.
- The screen split in two, each half turned towards its player.

## The world (first direction)
A diner table, two colours, one per player. **Signature:** the split screen and the loud, funny end card.

## How it would earn
- **Android:** a full-screen ad between sets of duels.
- **iPhone:** one-time unlock of more duels.

## Build order
1. One duel in a grey box, tested with two people at a real table.
2. Only if they laugh and ask for another: the other three duels and the evening score.

## What Anass does
Tries the one grey-box duel with a friend. If nobody laughs, we stop.

## Biggest risk
No gap, no edge. **Answer:** do not build it unless a twist is proven in the grey box.
