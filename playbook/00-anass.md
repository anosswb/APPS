# 00 — Anass: how he thinks, what he wants

Read this once at the start of a session. Everything here comes from his own words and calls in the Gobblo decision log (Nov 2026) and from his messages. When he shows you something new about himself, add it at the bottom with the date.

## The goal behind every request
He works 12-hour shifts in a hard physical job and he is tired. The apps are his way out. So:
- **Money is the point.** A beautiful app that cannot earn is a failure. Always know how this app earns before you design it.
- **His time is the scarcest thing.** He has maybe 1–2 good hours a day. Do the work yourself. Ask him only for what only he can do: a choice, a payment, a test on his phone.
- **He pays for tools with real money.** Do not waste tokens. Do not ask him to buy anything before he says he is ready.

## His market rule (13 Nov 2026)
**"No Arabic, no Darija. We target the US."** Apps are for American users, in English. Do not propose apps for Arabic speakers or built around Moroccan language or culture, even when it looks like an advantage. His advantage that counts is the work he knows: shifts, sites, machines.

## How he decides
- **He decides by looking, not by reading.** In Gobblo he changed the visual direction three times in two days, each time after seeing it on his phone. So show him early: 2–3 pictures or screens, not a long description. Expect him to change his mind after seeing, and plan for it (tokens, one config file, no hard-coded looks).
- **He picks from options he can see.** "3 directions as boards, wait for me to pick." "10 pictures, I choose."
- **He tests on his own phone and reports small real things.** ("Mom came in while the timer was running." "I heard only the vibration." "Soft keys are too loud.") Treat each of these as a real bug report.
- **He wants proof.** "Show me proof that you used each tool." Say what you ran and what you saw.
- **His call is final.** Push back once with a reason, then do it his way and log it.

## His taste (what he has rejected, in his words)
| He said | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| "It looks like a 5-minute vibe-coded app." | The default look of an AI is the enemy. See playbook/04. |
| "Pink, purple and black look childish." | No toy colours unless the users are children. |
| "The blob looked generic." | Characters and marks must be specific and have a reason to exist. |
| "Good icons, not just emojis." | Never use emoji as icons. |
| "Settings look like iPhone 6, not iOS 27." | Follow the current platform patterns. Check what the newest OS looks like. |
| "Dark mode looked flat and simple." | Dark mode is designed on its own, not inverted. |
| Clay "looks handmade, not AI." | For marketing art, prefer what looks made by a hand. |
| Rounded fonts dropped. | Rounded display type reads as a kids' app. |
| "Each app must have its special thing. Not the same design as Gobblo. Something better." (13 Nov 2026) | **Every app gets its own world and one signature.** Gobblo is a lesson, never a template. |

What he asks for, again and again: **top-10 App Store quality** ("Duolingo, Finch, Airbnb level"), rich detail, a product with personality, things that feel alive.

## His product instincts (they are good, use them)
- **Fast start, slow later:** "First levels fast, then slower, so people stay excited."
- **One tap to the value:** "A button, when you click it, the clock starts."
- **Do not make users feel bad.** He approved removing guilt words and the sad pet.
- **Keep what he loves:** "Don't remove anything." Before deleting a feature, ask.
- **No server, no AI bills in v1:** "Forget about AI." A feature that costs money per user needs a money plan first.
- **Speed:** "Keep going. Run jobs that touch different files side by side."

## His unfair advantages (use them when choosing ideas)
- He knows **12-hour shift work** from the inside. Most app designers never worked a night shift. (His repos also point to site and safety work, like toolbox talks. Ask him once which jobs he knows best, and write the answer here.)
- He already makes and sells **digital products** (printables, video content). He knows hooks, thumbnails and listings.
- He lives in **Morocco**, where costs are low, and sells to the **United States**, where users are worth the most. English is his second language, so a native US reader checks every store listing and first-run text before release.

## How he talks
Fast, direct, sometimes in capitals, no small talk. Match it: short answers, plain words, friendly. He treats you as a partner. Be one: tell him the truth, including "this idea will not make money, here is why".

## Things that annoy him
Long explanations · being asked the same thing twice · being asked to pay early · work that looks like every other AI app · losing something he liked · wasted tokens.
