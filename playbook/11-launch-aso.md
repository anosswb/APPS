# 11 — Launch, listing and store search (ASO)

Most installs for a small app come from store search. The listing is a product of its own: design it with the same care as the app.

## Name
- Short, easy for an American to say and spell after hearing it once, with no bad meaning in English or Spanish.
- Check before loving it: App Store and Google Play search, a trademark search (WIPO Global Brand Database, USPTO, EUIPO), the .app/.com domain, Instagram and TikTok handles. In Gobblo the first name was already taken by a new app; we found out before launch because we checked.
- The name is one constant in the code. Never typed in strings.

## Store search words
1. Collect real phrases from the US store's search dropdown (store country set to the United States).
2. Open the top 5 results for each phrase: are they huge companies (hard) or small makers with old apps (possible)?
3. Pick 1 main phrase and 3–5 side phrases where small apps rank.
4. Put them where each store reads them:
   - **iPhone:** title (30 characters), subtitle (30), keyword field (100, commas, no spaces, no repeats, no competitor names). The description is not searched.
   - **Android:** title (30), short description (80), and the long description, where the phrases appear naturally a few times.
5. Use American words and spelling ("color", "paycheck", "overtime", "truck"). A US native reads the title, subtitle and first screenshots before release.
6. Change one thing at a time and wait two weeks before judging.

## The listing page
- **Icon:** one shape, one idea, readable at the size of a fingernail. No text. Test it next to the competitors' icons in a row.
- **First 3 screenshots** do the selling (most people never scroll): 1. the special thing; 2. the main action; 3. the result or the feeling. Big caption of 3–6 words, real app screen, the app's own world. No prices, no fake reviews, no other brand names.
- **Preview video:** 15–25 seconds of real use, the best moment in the first 3 seconds.
- **Description:** first two lines say who it is for and the special thing. Then short lines. Honest.
- **US English first.** Other languages only later (playbook/09).
- Check the listing against playbook/08 before submitting.

## Launch order
1. **Weeks before:** Android closed test with the standing tester group (12 people, 14 days). iPhone TestFlight with 5–10 people. Fix what they find.
2. **Soft launch** in Canada, Australia or New Zealand for 1–2 weeks (English speakers who behave much like Americans). Watch day 1 return, crashes and the first reviews.
3. **Fix the first session** (playbook/06, part A7 or B).
4. **United States launch.** Other countries get the same English app for free reach.
5. **First 30 days:** answer every review, ship a small update every 1–2 weeks, post short videos.

## Ratings
The system prompt only, after a clear happy moment (a win, the third use), never in the first session, once per version. Reply to bad reviews with the fix. A rating under 4.0 hurts search more than anything else.

## Free growth that fits Anass
- **Short videos** (TikTok, Reels, Shorts): he already makes them. Show the special thing in 3 seconds; real screen recordings beat ads.
- **American communities** of the exact users (nurses, truckers, warehouse workers, puzzle fans on Reddit and in Facebook groups): ask for testers, do not spam.
- **Cross-promotion:** a quiet "More from us" row in each app's settings.
- **Seasonal pushes:** update the icon, screenshots and a small event before the season (playbook/09).

## What to measure in the store consoles
Impressions → page views → installs (conversion). Low impressions: keywords. Low page views: icon and title. Low installs from views: screenshots and rating. Compare by country.
